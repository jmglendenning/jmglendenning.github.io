EPPS 6302 Methods of Data Collection and Production: Assignment 6

Text analytics with quanteda

Author

John Glendenning

Published

October 19, 2026

What you will produce

Three corpora read with quanteda — archived summit tweets, presidential documents you collect from GovInfo, and the inaugural addresses — and a short report on what each kind of text can and cannot tell you, including what Wordfish measures.

0. Set up

  1. Install once, in the Console:
packages <- c(
  "quanteda",
  "quanteda.textstats",
  "quanteda.textplots",
  "quanteda.textmodels",
  "tidyverse"
)

# Check installation status
packages %in% rownames(installed.packages())
[1] TRUE TRUE TRUE TRUE TRUE

  1. Download quanteda_textanalytics01.R from Teams. Parts 1–3 below follow its three parts.

  2. You need the GovInfo key and the functions from govtdata01.R (Assignment 5) for Part 2.

1. Read

  1. quanteda — https://quanteda.io, and the tutorials at https://tutorials.quanteda.io, ch. 1–4 and 6.

Done

2. Analyze

  1. One summit, two kinds of text. In November 2021 Presidents Biden and Xi met by video.

    1. The public (Part 1). Tweets collected during the summit: frequent words, hashtag and mention networks, LSA. This dataset is archived — the API that produced it no longer offers that access, so it cannot be collected again.

    2. The government (Part 2). Collect every document in the Compilation of Presidential Documents (GovInfo, CPD) mentioning “Xi Jinping” since 20 January 2021. Read Xi in context with kwic(), then use keyness to compare the Biden and Trump administrations.

    3. Compare: what does each source let you say about the summit, and about US–China relations? Who produced each text, and for whom?

    4. Optional. For a public counterpart in 2025, collect YouTube comments on news coverage of the Trump–Xi meeting in Busan (30 October 2025) with your YouTube key, and compare with (i).

  2. US presidential inaugural addresses (Part 3), 1945 onward.

    1. Any similarities and differences over time and among presidents? Use length, lexical diversity, readability, cosine similarity, and keyness by party.

    2. Check which is the latest address in data_corpus_inaugural. If the 2025 address is missing, say how you would add it — and from which source.

3. What is Wordfish?

  1. Research it on the quanteda site (textmodel_wordfish()) and in tutorial ch. 6; rec. Slapin & Proksch (2008).

  2. Run it on the inaugural addresses (Part 3d). In a paragraph: what does the model assume about how words are generated? What does the dimension it found seem to measure, and what would you need to show that it does? Why must you set dir?

Note

Every corpus has a data-generating process. The tweets were chosen by a keyword and a collection window; the presidential documents by an editor at the Office of the Federal Register and by your query; the inaugurals by a tradition. Say which in your report — it decides what your keyness and Wordfish results can mean.
